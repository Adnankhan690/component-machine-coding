This is a really common trap — solving along with a solution (or right after watching one) trains **recognition**, not **recall**. Your brain goes "oh yeah, that makes sense" while watching, but recognition and being able to produce it cold from a blank file are two completely different skills. That gap is exactly what's causing the "I solved it once but I'm not sure I actually can" feeling.

The fix is a simple repetition system that forces retrieval instead of review:
1. **Attempt blind first**
   Before watching any solution, give yourself 15-20 min of genuine struggle on the problem. Even if you don't finish, this primes your brain to absorb the actual solution far more deeply than watching cold does.
2. **Solve along by typing, not copying**
   When you do watch a solution, pause after each key idea and write the code yourself in your own words. Say out loud why each line/hook exists before moving to the next part.
3. **Same-day blind rebuild**
   Close the solution, wait 1-2 hours, then try to rebuild it from just the problem statement with zero peeking. This is the first real test of whether it 'stuck' or just felt familiar.
4. **48-hour gap retry**
   Two days later, attempt the same question again from scratch with no notes open. If you get stuck, only peek at the specific part you're stuck on, not the full solution.
5. **7-day gap retry - the real test**
   A week later, attempt it cold one more time. If you can produce it smoothly here without having touched it in between, that's genuine, durable confidence, not short-term memorization.
6. **Vary one constraint**
   Once you can solve the original cold, change one thing, like debounce to throttle, flat array to tree data, or useState to useReducer, and solve that variant. This proves you understand the underlying pattern, not just the memorized code.
7. **Keep a simple [confidence log](file:///Users/adnan/Desktop/component-machine-coding/components/src/todos/confidence-log.md)**
   Track each question with 4 columns: Blind attempt, Solved with help, 48h retry, 7-day retry. Only mark a question as truly interview-ready once it clears the 7-day retry, not before.

**The one thing to change starting today:** if you're currently watching the solution *before* attempting the question, flip that order. Struggle first, even unsuccessfully — that struggle is what makes the solution stick when you see it.

For your Tier 1 list, this means slower throughput at first (maybe 2-3 questions actively cycling through these stages at once, rather than one-and-done), but by the time you're 3 weeks in, you'll have a smaller set of questions you can *actually* reproduce under pressure — which is worth far more in an interview than having "seen" 40 questions once each.

One more thing worth doing: when you're in the actual interview, you won't have a blank file and unlimited time either — so once a question clears its 7-day retry, try it once more with a 15-20 min timer to simulate real interview pressure.
